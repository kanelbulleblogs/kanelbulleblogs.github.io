>imasmiṃ sati idaṃ hoti, 
>
>imassuppādā idaṃ uppajjati; 
>
>imasmiṃ asati idaṃ na hoti, imassa nirodhā idaṃ nirujjhati
>
  
I just wanted to look cool in Pali  
Well, here’s the English version  

  
  
  
  
>When that is, this is
>
>This arises through arising of that
>
>When that is not there
>
>This isn’t too
>
>With cessation of that
>
>This ceases too
>

***- Pratītyasamutpāda***


I heard it in one of the lectures, some weeks ago. I didn’t know what to do with it or even what to make of it - so I just let it sit with me.
I followed up with different commentaries on it, did it help ? Well yes - at least to know that the Buddha implied causal effects , interdependence or co-dependent nature of things if plainly put. But then a part of the question still remains - what to make of this ?  

And then it happened, right in the middle of a breakdown - you know, when you grieve a version of yours from the past ? A life you lived - be it a hobby or a sport you took, a particular trait of yours you miss, a relationship you abandoned, a decision you never made… and then you resent, resent your present. 
But would it really help fix things by reclaiming a snapshot of yourself in time ?  

I remember studying deterministic functions in mathematics - given a set of variables in a function, you would always get a deterministic output.
We are not even the same person we went to sleep yesterday, so how would we expect these variables (in context of a lifetime) not to change and the result to be a constant?  

We are, through a differentials of time the version of ourselves today, because of an equal differentials of experiences , shaping these variables relative to time - the causal relationship. 
A today because a past of ours existed (I am stating the obvious and my English grammar teacher would mock me for the semantic blunders I made in the past).
Refuting your present would mean denying your past too, and so with it, the previous versions of yourself you took pride in.  

The Pratītyasamutpāda talks about co-dependence but not about co-existence. I am taking the liberty to attribute the Schrödinger’s cat - the logical absurdity that both versions of you would co exist in practicality.  

You have a free will to come up with yours.
